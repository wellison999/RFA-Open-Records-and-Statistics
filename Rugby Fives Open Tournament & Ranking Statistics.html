About the data

Things to bear in mind when reading the rankings, results and records on this site.

Rankings

  • The ranking method has changed over time. The current system (best three results per season, the last three seasons weighted 100% / 60% / 20%, ranking points = 10 × √ weighted total) was introduced in 2022/23. Earlier rankings were compiled differently, and the points scale has drifted over the decades, so compare positions rather than points between eras.
  • Before 2022/23 only the season-end rankings are shown (1981/82 onwards, read from the RFA's published lists). From 2022/23 there is a ranking after each tournament.
  • There were no rankings in 2020/21 (Covid). Season-by-season runs treat 2019/20 → 2021/22 as consecutive.
  • Some older ranking lists give initials only (e.g. "A Wynn"); these are matched to full names where the match is clear, and otherwise shown as printed.

Results

  • Results from 1998/99 to 2017/18 are read automatically from the RFA's annual Results and Reports booklets, with a few gaps filled from RFA news articles and players' own records. From 2018/19 they come from the RFA results database.
  • The archive is incomplete. Some seasons (notably 1999/00, 2003/04 and 2004/05) and some events were only partly reported, plates are often missing, and early-round names are sometimes surnames only. Round names are inferred by counting back from the final.
  • Players' names are matched across sources automatically (e.g. "W Ellison" = Will Ellison). A few may be merged or split wrongly; corrections are welcome.
  • Where a result is known but the score isn't, the match counts as a win or loss but is left out of the Elo ratings and score-based records.
  • The results grid covers the open tournaments only. The Scottish Open doubles is included from 2024/25, when it became a full open event again (before that it was a handicap competition). The South East Open doubles, played with drawn partners, isn't in the doubles grid.
  • Head-to-heads count main-draw matches only – plate matches are left out. U25 and University matches are also left out of head-to-heads and match records by default, because they aren't open events; use the toggle on the head-to-head page to include them.

Open titles, achievements and plates

  • Titles up to 2015/16 come from the RFA's record spreadsheets; later years from the RFA Champions Archive, and from 2018/19 from the results database, so new titles appear automatically. Where the database and the Champions Archive disagree, a short corrections list decides.
  • Only proper open events are counted: the National Singles and Doubles, the regional opens and the Puckle Cup. The South East Open doubles (drawn partners) and the Scottish Open doubles from 1991 to 2024 (President's Cup style) are left out; the Scottish doubles counts up to 1990 and again from 2025. U25, University, Winchester Fives and invitation events aren't titles here.
  • Seasons run from July to June. The National Singles was played in January until 1982 and in December after that.
  • Totals are checked against the RFA Hall of Fame.
  • Plate winners come from the season reports (1998/99 to 2017/18), the RFA's National Singles and Doubles plate lists, and plate finals in the results database. Many plates were never reported, so the list is incomplete.
  • Venues come from the contents pages and reports in the RFA's season reports (1998/99–2025/26), Will Ellison's results record and, for the National Singles and Doubles back to the 1920s, the RFA record books, kept in Archive\Titles\venues.txt – add a line there for each new tournament. Where a tournament was split across venues, none is shown.
  • Upsets use the Elo ratings going into each match, and only count when every player had at least five rated matches and six or more across the current and previous season – so newcomers' and returning players' ratings don't create false upsets.
  • Winchester Fives (the Barnes Bridge) has its own tab and a line on player profiles, but doesn't count towards rankings or head-to-heads. Barnes Bridge titles (12 points) and finals lost (5) do count on the Achievements board, along with a "two codes" badge.
  • Career clean sweeps follow the RFA record books' "grand slam": a player has won every open tournament that was available during their title-winning career. Events that hadn't started, had stopped, or only became open in the last couple of seasons (such as the Scottish doubles from 2025) don't count against them.
  • Achievement points are a bit of fun built from titles, National finals and season-end rankings. No player ages or dates of birth are used anywhere on this site.

Elo ratings

  • Elo ratings are unofficial and for interest only. They use every rated match since 1998/99, with more weight on best-of-three, 15-point and National matches. Shorter formats (games to 11, or a single game) also give the lower-rated player a better expected chance, as they are more of a lottery.
  • Players already in the season-end rankings when their first recorded match was played start from a rating based on their best ranking position in the previous five seasons rather than 1500, plus a bonus for each top-three finish in the previous ten seasons (up to +100) and +75 for rankings earned in seasons up to 1999/2000, when the fields were deeper. This lets the great players of the 1980s and 1990s, whose matches mostly predate the results archive, start at a level that reflects their record.
  • Ranking movements (Rankings → Movements) cover updates since 2022/23, when the current ranking method began, rebuilt from the latest ranking workbooks; where that differs from what was published, the bridge shows the difference as a "restated" step. See "How this page works" on that page.
  • Ratings decay towards 1500 for every calendar year a player doesn't play (see the Elo page). Peaks are never reduced.

Ranking movements

Every ranking update since the current method began in 2022/23 (best three results in each of the three weighted slots, 100% / 60% / 20%; points = 10 × √ weighted total). Pick two updates to see who moved and why, and a player to see their change as a bridge.

How this page works
  • Rebuilt history. Every ranking update since 2022/23 (when the current method began; earlier lists used a different system and can't be bridged) is rebuilt from the latest ranking workbooks: each player's points at each tournament, and which editions sat in the 100%, 60% and 20% slots at each update. Ranking points = 10 × √ (best three in each slot × 100% / 60% / 20%). The latest update matches the official list exactly.
  • Step by step. The bridge walks through every update between the two you pick. At each one it separates ageing (older editions moving down a slot, or dropping out, when a new edition arrives) from results (each edition new to the rankings since the previous update, including U25 and University events, which count but aren't updates of their own).
  • Best three. A result only adds to a player's total if it makes the best three in its slot; when it pushes another out, only the difference counts, and the step says which result it replaced. For the same reason ageing can be positive: a result outside the best three in the 100% slot can start counting once it moves into a slot with fewer results.
  • Ranking points or weighted total. Because of the square root, a point of weighted total is worth less to a player who already has a big total, and the split between steps depends a little on their order. The weighted-total view avoids both, adds up exactly and compares players like-for-like; the order of players is the same in both.
  • Published lists. Older updates can differ from what was published at the time, because results were later corrected, names merged or split, or tournament factors (which depend on the strength of the field) recalculated. Where the published figure for either update differs, the bridge starts or ends at the published figure with a "restated" bar for the difference, so it always ties back to the lists people saw. From West 2025 onwards the rebuild matches the published lists, apart from the Yorkshire 2026 corrections to South West and South East 2026.
  • Small print. Steps under 0.5 are grouped into "Other small changes", which also takes any rounding, so every bridge closes exactly. Ranks use unrounded points; the published lists sometimes order tied players differently.
